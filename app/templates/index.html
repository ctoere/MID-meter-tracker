A declaration of conformity is the strongest evidence this register can hold — it names the notified body and the models in scope, which a datasheet does not. It is also the most commonly over-read: a declaration covering "the EVSE" under the Low Voltage and EMC directives looks entirely official and says nothing about metrology. Only a declaration citing 2014/32/EU counts.
Work brand by brand, not row by row. One declaration usually lists a whole product family, so a single document can clear a dozen rows at once. The list below is ordered by how many rows each brand would settle. Searching is the manual part — there is no EU registry of declarations, so the tool builds the searches and reads what you bring back.

What gets extracted from each type

Datasheet

  • Brand, model, variant / article number
  • The exact sentence stating MID status
  • Meter make and model, if named
  • Accuracy class (EN 50470-3 A/B/C)
  • Document version and date

Nameplate photo

  • Brand, type, serial, article number
  • M-in-rectangle marking + year
  • Four-digit notified body number
  • Accuracy class, MI-003
  • Whether it's the meter or the enclosure

Conformity statement

  • Certificate / DoC number
  • Issuing notified body
  • Directive cited (2014/32/EU, MI-003)
  • Exactly which models it covers
  • Issue date and validity
A conformity statement is the strongest evidence available — stronger than a datasheet, because it names the notified body and the models in scope. It is also the most commonly over-read: a DoC covering "the EVSE" is not a MID certificate unless it cites 2014/32/EU. Worth checking rather than assuming.
